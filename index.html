Student-led research · Kenya & East Africa

The economics of everyday constraint.

Short, serious research on why development stalls at the level of the firm, the worker, and the household.

Uchimi began after a group of secondary-school students spent time in Kenya and kept returning to one question: why do so many capable people and businesses stay small and unproductive?

We read the literature, talk to people closer to the ground than we are, and publish careful notes on the microeconomic frictions behind persistent underdevelopment, informality, thin access to capital, labour-market frictions, and the cost of doing ordinary business.

The Wrong Instrument: Why Fertilizer Subsidies Have Not Closed Kenya's Maize Yield Gap

Kenya has subsidised fertilizer since 2008, and subsidy recipients do produce more. This paper argues that a planting-time price discount is the wrong instrument: the binding constraints are timing, soil quality and inframarginal spending, not price.

About the initiative

A small research group, not a think tank.

We are IB students who take these questions seriously. Uchimi exists to practise real economic reasoning, reading primary sources, handling data honestly, and writing clearly, on problems that matter rather than on textbook abstractions.

Our work is exploratory and openly provisional. We cite what we draw on, flag what we are unsure about, and treat every article as a draft that can be corrected. Read more about our approach →
